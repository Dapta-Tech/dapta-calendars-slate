# Display reads may be cached; decisions read the calendar fresh

A booking page shows a host's free times by reading the host's connected calendar. Every page view used to read it, through a pacer that keeps us under the calendar backend's per-host rate limit. Under a crowd that pacer became the bottleneck: twenty visitors opening one page queued twenty identical reads behind it, and the visible month took 9 s at the median and 27 s at worst on the development deployment. A calendar backend that failed or answered slowly took the page with it: the month came back empty after the backend's own retries, or after a 30 s timeout per request.

So display reads go through a short, per-connection cache in the API (`apps/api/src/busy-cache.ts`): a read answers every visitor of that calendar for 30 s, identical reads in flight collapse into one, a failed or slow read is answered by the connection's last good read if it is younger than 5 minutes, and a page never waits on a calendar for more than 10 s. Past that bound, or with nothing read yet, the connection's busy is unknown and the times are withheld with "this calendar can't be read right now", as before. The cache is per process; a booking written on one API replica clears that replica's entries for the hosts involved, and the others catch up within the TTL.

What makes this safe is a hard split, and it is the decision this record exists to keep. **Nothing that decides a booking reads through the cache.** A hold, a booking (personal, team, host-made, agent or v2), a reschedule and a host's confirmation each read the host's calendar fresh for the exact time, through the raw provider (`CalendarEffects.provider`), and refuse when it is busy (`SLOT_TAKEN` / `INVALID_SLOT`) or when it cannot be read (`CALENDAR_UNAVAILABLE`, 409 with `Retry-After`, nothing written). The cache sits behind a separate accessor (`CalendarEffects.displayProvider`), handed only to the reads that show times: the booking and team pages, the manage page's reschedule picker, the host's new-booking screen, and the slot reads of the machine and v2 surfaces. A time the cache shows that the calendar has since filled is therefore refused at the hold, which is the same thing that already happened when a time was taken between a page load and a click.

We considered caching inside `loadExternalBusy` with a freshness flag on every call. Rejected: the default would have to be chosen once for every caller, and the failure mode of a forgotten flag is a double booking. With two accessors the default is fresh, and a new caller has to reach for the cached one by name.

Consequences:

- A host's own calendar can briefly disagree with their booking page (up to 30 s on the replica that served it). Never with a booking: the write re-reads.
- During a calendar outage the page keeps showing the last known times for up to 5 minutes while every hold and booking is refused with a retryable error, so the visitor learns at the hold, not at the last step.
- `CALENDAR_BUSY_CACHE_TTL_MS=0` turns the cache off; the other knobs (`CALENDAR_BUSY_CACHE_MAX_STALE_MS`, `CALENDAR_BUSY_CACHE_MAX_ENTRIES`, `CALENDAR_BUSY_READ_TIMEOUT_MS`) are optional, and a bare fork with no calendar never builds one.
- Any future read that decides something (an agent's "is this time free?" that books on a yes, a reminder that checks for conflicts) reads through `provider`, never `displayProvider`.
